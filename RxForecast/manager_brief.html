Internal Brief — Supply Chain AI

RxForecast

A predictive EDI ordering agent for pharmacy chains — what it does, what data it runs on, and how the pieces fit together.

01 Overview

Mid-market pharmacy chains place 800–2,000+ purchase orders a week to drug wholesalers, still largely governed by static min/max shelf rules. Those rules break under three conditions: demand shocks, drug shortages, and controlled-substance/expiration edge cases — and a buyer ends up spending hours a day on manual exception handling instead of judgment calls. RxForecast replaces the static rule with a pipeline of purpose-built agents that forecast demand, watch for shortages, propose safe substitutes, and draft the actual purchase order — while a human buyer approves, edits, or rejects every single one.

It's built as a sequence of narrow, auditable stages rather than one large model call, because the decision itself is a sequence: forecast, reconcile against inventory, reason about a shortage if one exists, choose a distributor, draft the order, then watch for the wholesaler's response. Each stage's output — and the data that justified it — is logged, so a compliance officer can trace any purchase order back to the exact forecast, shortage bulletin, or buyer override that produced it.

What exists today is a working prototype, not the production system: real forecasting math, real EDI purchase-order generation, and a real audit trail, running against a synthetic dataset shaped like the real thing. Section 03 lays out exactly which pipeline stages are live logic today versus still spec.

03 System Architecture — The Agent Pipeline

Nine stages, executed nightly (plus on-demand for urgent shortages), each reading specific inputs and handing a specific output to the next stage. One human checkpoint sits in the middle — nothing reaches a wholesaler without buyer sign-off.

Deterministic / ML AI reasoning (LLM) Human-in-the-loop System / EDI transport Real & built in today's prototype Production spec, not yet live

Deterministic means the same input always produces the same output — ordinary math or a fixed rule, not a model forming a judgment. AI reasoning (LLM) means the stage weighs ambiguous, multi-factor evidence the way a person would, which is why those stages get the extra scrutiny in Section 04.

1 · Demand ForecasterLightGBM (prod) · trailing-avg (proto)

7-day SKU-store forecast with a confidence band; falls back to par-level guidance when a SKU has under six months of history.

LightGBM is a fast, well-established statistical forecasting model — not a language model. Deterministic given the same history, and cheap enough to re-run nightly for every SKU at every store.

IN · dispense history OUT · forecast + confidence band
2 · Shortage WatcherClaude, via Azure AI Foundry

Reads FDA and ASHP shortage bulletins and maps any active shortage onto the chain's own formulary. Rule-based stand-in in today's prototype.

IN · shortage bulletins OUT · shortage-linked SKUs
3 · Inventory ReconcilerDeterministic

Nets the forecast against on-hand and in-transit inventory, flags stockout risk, and suppresses SKUs already covered by an open order.

IN · forecastIN · on-hand / in-transit OUT · stockout-risk flag
4 · Substitution ReasonerClaude, escalates to Opus ~5%

Proposes a therapeutic alternative — Orange Book TE match, payer coverage, 340B eligibility — and hard-blocks on DEA Schedule II, no exceptions. Template logic in today's prototype.

TE match: the FDA's own rating that a substitute is clinically interchangeable. 340B eligibility: whether the store qualifies for a federal discount-pricing program on that drug. Escalates to Opus ~5%: the harder, more expensive model only steps in when the standard model's own confidence is low or two candidate substitutes conflict — most cases never need it.

IN · shortage-linked SKUIN · drug reference KB OUT · proposed substitute + rationale
5 · Sourcing OptimizerDeterministic

Chooses primary vs. secondary distributor by contract price, lead time, and allocation status.

IN · contracts & pricingIN · lead time OUT · distributor + quantity
6 · PO Drafter2-sigma sanity check

Assembles the draft order and checks the quantity against trailing 30-day order history before it ever reaches a buyer.

2-sigma sanity check: flags any quantity that falls outside the normal statistical range for that drug's order history — a fat-finger or edge-case quantity gets caught here automatically, before a human ever sees it, not after.

IN · sourcing decision OUT · draft order → buyer queue
Buyer Review — Human GateEvery order, no exceptions

Approve as recommended, edit the quantity, approve many at once, or reject with a reason. Nothing transmits without this step.

7 · EDI TransmissionX12 850 → wholesaler VAN

Generates the real X12 850 purchase-order document and sends it to the distributor.

IN · approved order OUT · X12 850 transmitted
8 · Exception HandlerWatches 855 / 856

Tracks the wholesaler's acknowledgment — accepted, backordered, partial allocation.

IN · 855 acknowledgment OUT · order status update

↺ On backorder or partial allocation, re-enters at Sourcing Optimizer (5)

Explainability LayerCross-cutting, every stage

Attaches a source citation to every stage's output as it runs — not a separate step, a wrapper on all of the above — so the compliance record is never reconstructed after the fact.

OutputWhat the chain actually gets

Ranked reorder queue for the buyer  ·  transmitted EDI 850 per approved order  ·  full audit trail with citations

04 Where We Are Today

Real, working now

  • Forecasting, inventory reconciliation, sourcing, PO drafting, exception handling — real logic, not scripted
  • Genuine X12 850/855 purchase-order generation
  • Complete audit trail with source citations, drillable per entry
  • Bulk order approval and role-based access for all five personas

Still ahead

  • Claude-powered reasoning for the Shortage Watcher and Substitution Reasoner (rule-based today)
  • Production cloud infrastructure (currently runs locally, in memory)
  • Live wholesaler connection in place of a simulated response
  • Enterprise SSO / MFA in place of the demo role-picker

Every stage from Section 03, laid out by exactly what it reads, exactly what it hands to the next stage, and whether it's genuinely agentic or could be — and today, mostly is — handled with ordinary deterministic logic. Where a stage reads more than one thing, each source is its own line; nothing here is summarized away.

StageInputsOutputsAgentic or Deterministic?

  • Distributor inventory feed — EDI 846
  • FDA Drug Shortage List & ASHP bulletins
  • Contract / GPO / 340B pricing sheets
  • Drug reference KB (NDC, Orange Book, RxNorm, DEA schedules)
  • Buyer override history

Deterministic

1 · Demand ForecasterDeterministic / ML
  • SKU-store-day dispense history
  • 7-day SKU-store forecast + confidence band
  • Falls back to par-level guidance under 6 months of history
Deterministic (ML)

A trained forecasting model — LightGBM in the production spec, a trailing-average in today's prototype — applied to historical numbers. Same history always produces the same forecast; nothing here is a judgment call.
2 · Shortage WatcherAI reasoning
  • FDA Drug Shortage List — polled every 4 hrs
  • ASHP bulletins — polled daily
  • Chain formulary — to map bulletins onto in-scope NDCs
  • List of shortage-linked SKUs, cross-checked against both sources
Agentic

Bulletins arrive as free text and press releases, worded differently every time — an LLM has to extract and normalize them, a fixed rule set can't keep up with the phrasing. Today's prototype substitutes a deterministic keyword-match rule set — it works against the known demo dataset but wouldn't generalize to a bulletin worded in a new way.
3 · Inventory ReconcilerDeterministic
  • Forecast — from stage 1
  • On-hand + in-transit inventory (EDI 846)
  • Open PO status — to suppress duplicate orders
  • Stockout-risk flag per SKU-store
Deterministic

Arithmetic: forecast minus on-hand minus in-transit, checked against a reorder-point threshold. A spreadsheet formula could do this.
4 · Substitution ReasonerAI reasoning
  • Shortage-linked SKU — from stage 2
  • Orange Book TE codes, RxNorm, NDC directory, DEA schedules
  • Payer coverage & 340B eligibility
  • Contract / formulary availability
  • Proposed substitute NDC + rationale
  • Hard-blocked, no output, on DEA Schedule II — no exceptions
Agentic

Weighs several independent, sometimes conflicting factors at once — TE match, payer coverage, 340B eligibility, contract availability — a combinatorial judgment call, not a lookup table. One part is always deterministic regardless: the DEA Schedule II block is hard-coded logic, never delegated to the model — a safety rule that must never have exceptions shouldn't depend on a probabilistic system.
5 · Sourcing OptimizerDeterministic
  • GPO / 340B / direct contract pricing
  • Distributor lead time — historical 850→856 deltas
  • Allocation status
  • Chosen distributor + order quantity
Deterministic

A cost / lead-time / allocation comparison across a known, finite list of distributors — picks whichever satisfies the constraints at lowest cost.
6 · PO DrafterSystem
  • Sourcing decision — from stage 5
  • Trailing 30-day order history — for the 2-sigma sanity check
  • Draft purchase order, queued for buyer review
Deterministic

Assembles the order from the sourcing decision and runs the 2-sigma statistical check against trailing order history — templating and arithmetic, no judgment.
Buyer Review — Human GateHuman-in-the-loop
  • Draft order — from stage 6
  • Approved as-is, edited, bulk-approved, deferred, or rejected with a reason
Neither — human decision

Not an agent at all. A person approves, edits, or rejects. This is the one step the system deliberately does not automate.
7 · EDI TransmissionSystem
  • Buyer-approved order
  • Transmitted X12 850 purchase-order document, sent to the distributor
Deterministic

Formats the approved order into the X12 850 segment structure and assigns the next control number in sequence — a protocol/templating task, not a decision.
8 · Exception HandlerDeterministic
  • X12 855 acknowledgment
  • X12 856 advance ship notice
  • Order status update — accepted, backordered, or partial allocation
  • On backorder: re-enters the pipeline at stage 5, Sourcing Optimizer
Deterministic

A fixed state machine keyed on the 855's status code — accepted, backordered, or partial — routing to a replan only on the backorder branch. A decision table, not reasoning.
Explainability LayerCross-cutting
  • Every stage's own output, as it's produced
  • A source citation attached to each output, accumulated into the audit trail
Deterministic

A wrapper that mechanically attaches whatever citation each stage already produced to the run record — it doesn't generate new judgment of its own.
